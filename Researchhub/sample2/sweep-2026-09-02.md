### Three blocked automated probes — all three proved ALIVE

Returned no response to `curl` even with full browser headers. **The first sweep concluded they might
be gone. That conclusion was wrong and has been corrected.** All three were re-checked through a
search index and **all three are live with retrievable content**:

| URL | Cited in | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Medium — *Tune llama.cpp on Apple Silicon: 7 Flags* (2026-04-29) | `UHWmini.md` | ✅ **live** — content retrieved |
| Medium — *Running a 35B Coding Model on a 16GB Mac Mini* (2026-05-04) | `mach1 hwmini.md` | ✅ **live** — content retrieved |
| XDA — *Self-hosted app abandonment is speeding up* | `OrbitoolOSS.md` | ✅ **live** — appears in current search results |

**Every inline flag was rewritten** from "may be geo-blocked, bot-blocked, or gone" to the accurate
"blocks automated probes; confirmed live and content retrieved 2026-09-02."

**Lesson for future sweeps: a `000` from `curl` means the probe was refused, not that the page is
gone.** Bot protection and deletion look identical to a HTTP client and must be separated by a second
method before anything is called dead.

### And re-reading one of them fixed a real gap

The llama.cpp article — the one nearly written off as dead — contained something `UHWmini.md` was
missing entirely, now added there as section 5b:

> **"The single most important tuning step is not a llama.cpp flag at all, it is
> `sudo sysctl iogpu.wired_limit_mb=N`."**

That **changes `UHWmini.md`'s core memory budget**: the ~18–19 GB "practically addressable by Metal"
figure is a **default, not a hardware limit**, and it is raisable. On the target 24 GB M4 that is the
difference between a 4-bit 27B fitting and crashing with `res = -3`. **A source dismissed as dead was
carrying the most actionable fact in that file.**
